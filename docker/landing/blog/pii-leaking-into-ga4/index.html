Personal data is leaking into GA4. Here is what we found.

Analytics is where personal data goes to be forgotten. Nobody means to send an email address to Google Analytics — it just rides along in a URL, a form field, or a search query that someone wired into an event parameter two years ago.

We pointed Voidwall at the outbound analytics traffic of twelve production properties for two weeks and counted what crossed the wire.

What we found

Across the twelve properties:

  • Nine sent at least one event carrying an email address, usually inside a page path or a search_term parameter.
  • Five leaked phone numbers, almost always from un-sanitised form submissions fired as custom events.
  • Every one forwarded a full client IP somewhere in the request, despite IP anonymisation being "on."

The common thread: none of it was deliberate. It was the residue of features shipped quickly and instrumented generously.

Why it happens

GA4's model encourages you to attach context to events. Context is exactly where PII hides:

  1. A checkout page with the email in the URL for "convenience."
  2. A site-search box whose query becomes a tracked parameter.
  3. A support widget that helpfully includes the user's phone in its payload.

Each one is reasonable in isolation. Together they turn your analytics pipeline into an uncontrolled copy of your customer database.

What actually fixes it

You can audit event schemas by hand, but schemas drift and new events appear weekly. The durable fix is to inspect the traffic itself, on its way out, and redact what shouldn't leave:

  • Strip emails and phone numbers from parameter values before they reach the collector.
  • Truncate or drop client IPs at the wall, not at the vendor's mercy.
  • Keep a count of what was redacted, by kind, so you can see the leak shrink.

The numbers don't have to be pretty. They have to be visible — and then they have to go to zero.
